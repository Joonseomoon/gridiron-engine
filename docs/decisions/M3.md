## M3 shared prerequisite: benchmark and correctness (2026-11-05)

21.0.0, from the repo root). A change that alters games bumps `kModelVersion`, passes a statistical check instead, and

### Benchmark method

- Release build only (`-DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release`); unoptimized builds are about 5.7× slower.
- `simulate BAL SEA --games 1000000 --seed 1000`, output discarded; 1 warm-up, then 7 runs with `/usr/bin/time -p`;
  report the median `real` and games/s. Shorter runs hide clock slowdown under sustained load (below).
- Profiles: `build-profile/` (Release plus `-g -fno-omit-frame-pointer`), macOS `sample`, one thread.

### Developer A baseline (`1595012`)

MacBook Pro, Apple M5 (4 performance + 6 efficiency cores), 32 GB, macOS 26.6.2, Apple clang 21.0.0.

| Threads | Median s | Range s | Games/s | Speedup |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 12.30 | 12.23–12.57 | 81,301 | 1.00× |
| 2 | 6.25 | 6.19–6.39 | 160,000 | 1.97× |
| 4 | 3.51 | 3.47–3.61 | 284,900 | 3.50× |
| 8 | 2.25 | 2.22–2.36 | 444,444 | 5.47× |
| 10 | 2.20 | 2.14–2.41 | 454,545 | 5.59× |

- One thread matches the runner before M3.1 B (`0c38ad2`: 81k games/s), so threading costs nothing at T = 1.
- Scaling is near-perfect to 2 threads. At 4 it is 3.9× on 100,000-game runs but 3.5× here, and one 4-thread
  pass slowed run after run (3.15 → 3.52 s): the chip lowers its clock under sustained all-core load.
- Past 4 threads the work lands on the slower efficiency cores, and the runner's equal split makes the fast cores wait
  for them: 8 → 10 threads adds almost nothing. Dynamic scheduling is the M3.2 A target.

Profile (one thread, 1,000,000 games, 8,587 samples, self time; same shape at `0c38ad2`):

| Function | Share |
| --- | --- |
| `std::normal_distribution` | 30.7% |
| `log` (inside normal sampling) | 19.1% |
| `applyOutcome` | 16.9% |
| `samplePlayOutcome` | 11.0% |
| `choosePlayType` (includes inlined `mt19937`) | 8.5% |
| `runBatch` (mostly `mt19937` seeding per game) | 6.6% |
| `runGame` | 3.3% |
| Run, pass, and field goal samplers | 4.0% |

**Per-play `GameState` copy.** No allocation or `std::string` call appears, because "BAL" and "SEA" fit in libc++'s
inline string buffer. Renaming BAL to 31 characters forces an allocation per copy: 1.63 s vs 1.22 s per 100,000 games
(+34%), with `malloc`, `free` and `memmove` at 24% of samples, so the whole state is copied every play. With short
names the copy costs an estimated 5–10%. It is the largest hot spot that can change without changing output.

### M3.1 A target: normal-distribution reuse (decided 2026-11-05)

- **Decision:** M3.1 A optimizes normal sampling instead of the copy; the copy moves to M3.2 B.
- **Reason:** normal sampling is about 50% of run time. libc++ generates normal values in pairs and keeps the second in
  the distribution object, but the samplers build a new object per draw, so half the work (uniform draws, `log`,
  `sqrt`) is thrown away.
- **Consequence:** the seed → game mapping changes, a deliberate exception to ROADMAP 3.1's "without changing its public
  behavior". It needs a model-version bump, new fingerprints, and a statistical check. The distribution must be created
  per game, never per thread or `static`, so game i depends only on seed i at every thread count.

## M3.1 Developer A: normal-distribution reuse (2026-11-05)

`RandomSource` (`src/engine/RandomSource.h`) holds a reference to the game's `mt19937` and one
`std::normal_distribution<double>`. `runGame` creates it once per game; every sampler takes it in place of the rng and
passes `{mean, sd}` per draw.

**Timing** (A's machine, one thread, method above):

| Revision | Median s | Range s | Games/s | Speedup |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before (`1595012`) | 12.30 | 12.23–12.57 | 81,301 | 1.00× |
| After (`e4dfb8a`) | 9.12 | 9.09–9.24 | 109,649 | 1.35× |

26% less time, against an estimate of 20–25% (half of a ~50% share).

**Profile after** (`e4dfb8a`, one thread, 1,000,000 games, 6,872 samples). "Time" is share × median run time, so rows
compare directly with the baseline:

## M3.2 Developer B: remove per-play `GameState` copies (2026-11-07)

`applyOutcomeInPlace(GameState&, const PlayOutcome&)` updates the caller's state and returns a `PlaySummary` (event,
turnover, period). The rules and clock logic live there. `applyOutcome` stays as a wrapper that copies its input, calls
the in-place version, and returns a `PlayResult`; the separate name keeps overload resolution unchanged for existing
callers. `runGame` uses the in-place version, so batches make no per-play copies. With an observer it saves the
before-state and builds a `PlayResult`, keeping the log unchanged. The model stays `m3-v1`.

**Correctness** (`ecaefa3`): the `m3-v1` fingerprints match, with the terminal hash identical at 1, 2, 4, 8, 10 threads.
The play-by-play log matches the 3.1 code on 8 seeds. All 209 tests pass in Release, warnings-as-errors, and ASan/UBSan
builds; clang-format is clean.

**Timing** (Apple M1 Pro, 8 cores, 16 GiB, macOS 27.2, Apple clang 21.0.0; one thread, method above):

| Revision | Median s | Range s | Games/s | Speedup |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before (`71c1f7a`) | 16.59 | 16.58–16.74 | 60,277 | 1.00× |
| After (`8893412`) | 14.72 | 14.64–14.84 | 67,935 | 1.13× |

11.3% less time, against an estimate of 5–10%. The ranges do not overlap.

## M3.2 Developer A: scheduling investigation (2026-11-08)

Measured whether the equal split leaves cores idle, using temporary per-worker finish times (not committed). A's machine,
`ecaefa3`, 10 threads, 1,000,000 games. "Possible saving" is `1 − mean ÷ last`, the most perfect balancing could recover:

| Run | First finish ms | Last finish ms | Mean finish ms | Possible saving |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1022 | 1239 | 1179 | 4.8% |
| 2 | 1032 | 1208 | 1174 | 2.8% |
| 3 | 1100 | 1243 | 1191 | 4.2% |
| 4 | 1151 | 1195 | 1175 | 1.7% |
| 5 | 1138 | 1255 | 1197 | 4.6% |

- **Decision (superseded by the chunk scheduling section below):** keep the equal split unless the M3.2 A
  chunk-scheduling experiment shows a gain beyond run-to-run spread.
- **Reason:** the possible saving averages 3.6% and stayed under the agreed 5% cutoff in every run, and a real scheduler
  would recover only part of it. Most workers finish together, despite 4 fast and 6 slow cores, because macOS moves
  threads between cores. The earlier 10–13% headroom estimate was too high, so the gap to ideal scaling is mostly not
  imbalance.
- **Consequence:** revisit if work items start to vary in cost, as M4's continuations from different situations may.

## M3.2 Developer A: `parallelFor` and worker exceptions (2026-11-08)

`parallelFor(count, threads, work)` now owns block splitting, thread start, and joins; `runBatch` only says how to run a
block. Each worker catches into its own `std::exception_ptr`. After all joins the lowest-numbered worker's exception is
rethrown, so the choice doesn't depend on timing; the other workers finish first. `main` prints `error: <message>` and
exits 1 for any `std::exception`. The model stays `m3-v1`.

**Correctness** (`c1d5178`): the terminal fingerprint matches at 1, 2, 4, 8, 10 threads. All 215 tests pass in
warnings-as-errors, ThreadSanitizer, and ASan/UBSan builds. With the worker `try/catch` removed, the new tests crash
(exit 134), so they do detect the bug.

**Timing** (A's machine, macOS 27.2, 1,000,000 games, method above, with before and after alternated each round):

| Threads | Before (`3db1209`) median s | Range s | After (`c1d5178`) median s | Range s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 8.11 | 8.10–8.12 | 8.11 | 8.10–8.13 |
| 10 | 1.27 | 1.21–1.35 | 1.29 | 1.23–1.40 |

No measurable cost: identical at 1 thread, and the 10-thread ranges overlap almost entirely.

## M3.2 Developer A: chunk scheduling (2026-11-08)

Workers claim blocks of 64 games from a shared `std::atomic<int>` as they finish, instead of taking equal shares up
front. Each chunk has its own exception slot, and the earliest failing chunk's exception is rethrown, so the choice still
doesn't depend on timing.

**Timing** (A's machine, 1,000,000 games, on top of `c1d5178`, builds rotated each round; 15 rounds at 10 threads, 7 at
1 thread; output hash unchanged for every build):

| Build | Threads | Median s | Range s | CPU s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equal split | 10 | 1.31 | 1.27–1.43 | 11.94 |
| Chunk 1 | 10 | 1.27 | 1.22–1.39 | 12.03 |
| Chunk 64 | 10 | 1.26 | 1.21–1.29 | 12.00 |
| Chunk 1024 | 10 | 1.27 | 1.22–1.32 | 11.99 |
| Chunk 4096 | 10 | 1.28 | 1.24–1.36 | 11.95 |
| Equal split | 1 | 8.10 | 8.10–8.15 | 8.10 |
| Chunk 64 | 1 | 8.11 | 8.09–8.14 | 8.10 |

- **Decision:** keep chunk scheduling with chunk size 64. The rule was changed from "keep if faster than the spread" to
  "keep if not slower", because it also protects against games of uneven cost in M4.
- **CI proof:** the ThreadSanitizer job's failure on a planted race was proven locally only (exit 134), not in CI.
- **Reason:** chunk 64 had the best median, about 4% under the equal split, matching the 3.6% measured headroom. The
  ranges overlap, but the equal-split run was the slower one in 97% of run pairs. Sizes 1 to 4096 are within 0.02 s of each other. 64 was chosen over larger sizes because a batch
  smaller than one chunk runs on a single thread.
